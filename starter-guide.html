WELCOME TO PLENIORA

Your shop setup, step by step.

Use this guide to understand the modules and set up the business manager. A good first setup connects products, stock, purchases, sales and accounts, so your reports make sense.

1. Create your account and shop

  1. Choose Start free trial and enter your name, shop name, email and a strong password.
  2. Your 30-day trial begins on signup. Sign in with the email and password you registered.
  3. Open Business Setup to confirm your business name, type and currency.
  4. Use Customization to add the product categories, measurement units, payment methods and staff roles that match your shop.

2. Add products and opening stock

Open Products & Stock → Add Product. Enter a clear product name, SKU, category, fabric/type, color/design, size, unit, purchase price, selling price, current quantity and low-stock threshold. Use a unique SKU or barcode for faster POS search.

For a physical stock count, enter opening quantities or use inventory controls to adjust quantity. Keep product units consistent (for example, all fabric quantities in meters).

3. Make a sale

Open Point of Sale (or Daily Sales → New Sale in the older local workflow). Search by SKU/barcode/name, add products to the cart, check quantities, choose a customer, enter any discount/tax, choose one or two payment methods and amounts, then complete the sale. The system reduces stock and stores the invoice. Use the print action to print a customer receipt.

Select a customer for credit sales so the amount due can be followed in the customer account ledger. Review stock before finalizing a sale.

4. Record purchases and suppliers

Add a supplier, then record a purchase with products, quantities, purchase prices and amount paid. The purchase increases stock. Unpaid amounts are recorded as supplier payables. Use Ledgers & Accounts to record supplier payments and review balances.

5. Follow customers, expenses and cash

Add customers and set their credit details. Record customer receipts from Ledgers & Accounts to reduce outstanding balances. Add operating expenses such as rent or transport under Expenses. Open a cash register at shift start, record cash-in/out and close it with the counted cash at shift end.

Sales returns and purchase returns should be recorded in Returns so stock movement and accounts reflect the return.

6. Set up staff

Add employees with position and salary details. Use attendance actions to record present, absent or leave. Record salary payments or advances so the expense and employee payment history stay together. In Admins and Customization, create user accounts and choose role permissions carefully.

7. Read the dashboard and reports

The dashboard summarizes sales, estimated profit, stock value and low-stock products. Reports summarize sales, gross profit, expenses and net profit. Alerts highlight low stock, customer receivables, supplier payables and tasks. Check purchase prices and expense entries regularly because incorrect costs affect profit.

8. Back up your records

Download a JSON backup from Settings & Backup before important changes. Store the backup somewhere safe. Restoring replaces current data, so use it only when you intend to recover that backup. Ask your service administrator about exports and data retention.

Trial, subscription and privacy: This starter backend stores business records on the server and keeps account passwords hashed. Trial access lasts 30 days. Monthly/yearly plan selection is present, but live charges require configured prices, a payment gateway and webhook validation. Password reset delivery requires an email service. Use HTTPS, secure server backups and a production database before serving real customer data on a public marketplace.

Restaurant: tables and orders

  1. Add menu items as products and set each item price and available quantity.
  2. Open Tables & Orders, add your floor tables, or start a takeaway ticket.
  3. Add dishes to a ticket and send it to the kitchen queue. Move each order through Preparing, Ready and Served.
  4. Choose Bill to bring the served ticket to Point of Sale, collect payment and record the sale.

Restaurant items use the same product quantity as stock. Recipe ingredients and automatic ingredient consumption are not included yet.
